## The moment the day turned

He explained why lines 2–4 didn't print in Ex 7 with: *"Python is an interpreted language, it stopped on the error."* Right for Ex 7, wrong for Ex 6 — and the gap was worth chasing rather than correcting.

So I had him predict and run two experiments instead of telling him:

- **A** — syntax error moved to line 4. Predicted "3 lines print." **Nothing printed.**
- **B** — `prnt` moved to line 4. Predicted "nothing prints." **3 lines printed, then the error.**

**Both predictions inverted.** His reaction: *"ohh gosh I was wrong in both the attempts!"*

That is the best thing that happened all day. Being wrong in both directions is what made the two-pass model stick — by check Q4 he had it cold. **Repeat this pattern: predict first, then run, especially where the prediction is likely wrong.**

## What he found hard

**The nature of pass 2.** In Q1 he described it as "goes line by line for NameErrors" — a second *checking* pass. It is **execution**. `NameError` is not what pass 2 hunts for; it is one thing that can go wrong while pass 2 does the actual work.

Costs little today. Matters from D2 onward, when variables start holding state that execution actually changes. **D2 opens with a five-minute correction of exactly this, then proceeds.** Logged as `meta.remediation`.

Minor, not penalised: he attributed `NameError` to misspelled *keywords*. Misspelling a real keyword generally yields `SyntaxError`. Sharpen during D2.
