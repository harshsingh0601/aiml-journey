## What was taught

One concept, taught C#-forward — he is a senior engineer, not a non-programmer, so
assignment itself needed no explaining. The teaching went into what Python *removes*:

- `=` is an instruction, not a claim. **Right side first, then the label.**
- No type declaration, no `;`, no declaration-vs-assignment split
- **The name has no type; the value does.** `city = "Pune"` then `city = 42` is legal —
  the single biggest day-one difference from C#
- Assignment hands over a value, it does not link two names (`y = x`, then `x` changes, `y` doesn't)
- Naming: enforced rules vs snake_case convention
- **Keywords vs builtin names** — `class = 5` is `SyntaxError`; `print = 5` is legal and quietly
  destroys `print()`
- `type()`

Not touched, by design: mutability, aliasing, lists, functions, loops. Names-vs-objects is
**D8** and was explicitly named as out of bounds at the open.
